Enhanced ABM: Demographics & Churn Analysis

May 3, 2026 | 12 min read

This post is a continuation of my earlier work on Agent-Based Modeling: Simulating Social Feature Adoption. In that project, I built a basic model where 1,000 users on a 50x50 grid adopted a social media feature based solely on how many of their neighbors had already done so. It captured network effects well, but it had two major simplifications: all users were identical, and once someone adopted, they never left. This enhanced model fixes both of those.

What's new in this version:
  • Realistic demographic segmentation — age groups and tech savviness
  • Churn mechanics — users can abandon features after adoption
  • 100 Monte Carlo simulations for statistical robustness
  • Actionable business insights from demographic patterns

Model Enhancements Overview

The core change is transforming simple, identical agents into realistic users with individual characteristics that affect their behavior. Here's how the two versions compare:

Feature Basic Model Enhanced Model
Demographics None (all identical) Age group + Tech savviness
Influence Threshold Uniform(0.20, 0.60) Modified by demographics
Adoption Permanent Can churn after adoption
Tracking Adoption only Adoption + Churn + Demographics
Analysis Single run 100 Monte Carlo simulations

Demographics Implementation

Age Groups

Users are segmented into three age categories, each with distinct behavioral patterns. Younger users are quicker to adopt but also quicker to leave. Older users are harder to convince but far more loyal once they're in.

Age Group Distribution Threshold Modifier Churn Modifier Behavior
Young (18–30) 30% -0.10 1.5x Quick to adopt, quick to leave
Middle (31–55) 50% 0.00 1.0x Baseline behavior
Senior (56+) 20% +0.10 0.7x Slower to adopt, more loyal

Tech Savviness

Technical proficiency is modeled as a three-tier system that independently affects both adoption resistance and retention:

Tech Level Distribution Threshold Modifier Churn Modifier Characteristics
Low 20% +0.15 1.3x High resistance, high churn
Medium 50% 0.00 1.0x Baseline behavior
High 30% -0.15 0.8x Low resistance, low churn

Combined Threshold Calculation

Each agent's influence threshold is computed by combining a base random value with both demographic modifiers:

threshold = clip(base_threshold + age_modifier + tech_modifier, 0.05, 0.95)

This creates meaningful heterogeneity. A young, high-tech user might have a threshold of 0.15 — easy to convince with minimal social proof. A senior, low-tech user might land at 0.65 — needing two-thirds of their neighbors on board before adopting.

Churn Mechanism

How It Works

After initial adoption, each user enters a 10-day "honeymoon period" of guaranteed retention. After that, every day they face a probability of abandoning the feature:

P(churn) = base_rate × age_modifier × tech_modifier

The base churn rate is 2% per day. Once a user churns, they do not re-adopt. This creates meaningful differences in retention across the user base:

Segment Daily Churn Rate Expected Retention at 30 Days
Young + Low Tech 3.9% ~30%
Middle + Medium Tech 2.0% ~55%
Senior + High Tech 1.4% ~65%

Impact on System Dynamics

Churn fundamentally changes the shape of the adoption curve. Without churn, the model from the original post showed adoption climbing monotonically to a plateau. With churn, the system behaves differently:

  • Without churn: Adoption increases steadily to a plateau and stays there
  • With churn: The system reaches equilibrium where new adoptions balance losses
  • Peak occurs: Around the point where adoption rate exceeds churn rate
  • Equilibrium: Settles at roughly 40–50% of the population as active users

Statistical Analysis: 100 Simulations

A single simulation run is always subject to the randomness of initial conditions — which users are early adopters, how the grid is populated. To get reliable estimates, I ran 100 independent Monte Carlo simulations, each with:

  • 1,000 agents with randomly assigned demographics
  • 5% seeded as early adopters
  • 300 simulated days
  • Full daily tracking of adoption, churn, and demographic breakdowns

Variability

The coefficient of variation for final active users was approximately 11%, indicating moderate variability from random initial conditions. The 90% confidence interval spans 360 to 540, which provides a reliable planning range for business use cases.

Business Implications

Strategic Insights

  • Target middle-aged, high-tech users for stable growth: These users balance solid adoption rates with low churn, forming the reliable core of the user base.
  • Invest in retention for young users: They drive early momentum, but without ongoing retention efforts, that momentum won't hold.
  • Simplify UX for low-tech users: This segment faces two barriers at once — high resistance to adoption and high churn if they do. Better onboarding could unlock a meaningful market segment.
  • Plan for equilibrium, not continuous growth: With churn, the system stabilizes around 40–50% active users. Revenue and infrastructure projections should reflect a plateau, not exponential growth.
  • Prepare early for peak: Maximum engagement arrives around Day 85. Marketing campaigns, server capacity, and support resources should be in place before then.

Risk Factors

  • High variability in outcomes: Random initial conditions can produce 20–30% variance in final user counts across otherwise identical setups.
  • Churn significantly erodes long-term value: Without retention efforts, churn reduces the final user base by 30–40% compared to no-churn scenarios.
  • Demographic imbalances: A user base skewed young and low-tech will have structurally higher churn that's hard to overcome without product changes.
  • Network effects can fail to launch: If early adopters are poorly connected or clustered in low-density grid regions, adoption may stall before reaching critical mass — the same stagnation issue from the original model, now compounded by churn.

Technical Implementation

The model was built in Python using the same Mesa framework from the original project, with additional libraries for statistics and visualization:

  • Mesa (v2.x): Agent-based modeling framework
  • NumPy: Numerical computing and random number generation
  • Pandas: Data manipulation and statistical analysis
  • Matplotlib: Time series plots and distribution charts
  • tqdm: Progress tracking for the 100-simulation batch run
  • Google Colab: Cloud execution environment

The implementation is organized into modular components:

class EnhancedSocialMediaUser(mesa.Agent):
    def __init__(self, model):
        super().__init__(model)
        # Assign age group
        self.age_group = np.random.choice(
            ['young', 'middle', 'senior'], p=[0.30, 0.50, 0.20]
        )
        age_modifiers = {'young': (-0.10, 1.5), 'middle': (0.00, 1.0), 'senior': (0.10, 0.7)}
        age_thresh_mod, self.age_churn_mod = age_modifiers[self.age_group]

        # Assign tech savviness
        self.tech_level = np.random.choice(
            ['low', 'medium', 'high'], p=[0.20, 0.50, 0.30]
        )
        tech_modifiers = {'low': (0.15, 1.3), 'medium': (0.00, 1.0), 'high': (-0.15, 0.8)}
        tech_thresh_mod, self.tech_churn_mod = tech_modifiers[self.tech_level]

        base = np.random.uniform(0.20, 0.60)
        self.influence_threshold = np.clip(base + age_thresh_mod + tech_thresh_mod, 0.05, 0.95)

        self.adopted_feature = False
        self.churned = False
        self.days_since_adoption = 0

    def step(self):
        if self.churned:
            return

        if not self.adopted_feature:
            neighbors = self.model.grid.get_neighbors(self.pos)
            if neighbors:
                adoption_rate = sum(n.adopted_feature for n in neighbors) / len(neighbors)
                if adoption_rate > self.influence_threshold:
                    self.adopted_feature = True
        else:
            self.days_since_adoption += 1
            if self.days_since_adoption > 10:  # honeymoon period
                base_churn = 0.02
                p_churn = base_churn * self.age_churn_mod * self.tech_churn_mod
                if np.random.random() < p_churn:
                    self.adopted_feature = False
                    self.churned = True

Running 100 simulations of 1,000 agents over 300 time steps took approximately 5–10 minutes on a CPU-only Google Colab instance, generating roughly 30 MB of time-series data in total.

Conclusion

Adding demographics and churn to the original model transforms it from a theoretical toy into something closer to a real business planning tool. The key insight is that not all users behave the same — and the mix of who adopts early has compounding effects on long-term retention.

Key takeaways:
  • Demographics and churn shift the system from monotonic growth to dynamic equilibrium
  • 100 simulations provide confidence intervals useful for actual business planning
  • Young/low-tech segments need different product strategies than middle-aged/high-tech ones
  • Peak engagement (~Day 85) happens well before the system stabilizes — prepare for it early

Future directions I'm interested in exploring: modeling retention campaigns, replacing the grid with a small-world or scale-free network topology, simulating competitive dynamics between two features, and tracking cohort lifetime value over time.
